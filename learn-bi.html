What is a BI system?

If your CEO ever asked "where do these numbers come from?", this guide is for you.

The 30-second answer

A BI (Business Intelligence) system is the chain of tools that takes raw events from your business - every order, every login, every cost - and turns them into the dashboards and reports your team uses to make decisions.

Said differently: it's the difference between someone asking "what's our revenue this month?" and getting an answer in 2 seconds versus 2 days.

The four pieces every BI system has

  1. Sources. Your CRM, e-commerce platform, ad accounts, application database. Anywhere events happen - that's a source.
  2. Pipelines. The scheduled jobs that pull data from each source automatically. They retry on failure, alert when broken, and run while you sleep.
  3. Warehouse. A central database where clean, modeled data lives. Think BigQuery or Snowflake - the single place every dashboard reads from.
  4. Dashboards. The visual layer your team actually opens every morning. Power BI, Tableau, Looker, Metabase - whichever your team will use.

Why a BI system - not just spreadsheets?

Spreadsheets work great until the business grows. Then three people are emailing different versions of "the same" report, no one knows which is right, and two hours per week disappear into reconciliation.

A BI system fixes this:

  • One source of truth. One number, valid for everyone.
  • Auto-refresh. No more "hold on, let me update it."
  • Scales with you. Works with 10 new sources and 100 more dashboards.
  • Trustworthy. Every number traces back to the row that produced it.

Common pitfalls

  • Building before defining. If there's no agreement on "what is revenue", the dashboard will contradict itself within a week.
  • Tool sprawl. A company with 7 different BI tools spends more time on coordination than on insights.
  • No documentation. Every metric should be defined once, in one place. If it's not, every team will reinvent it differently.
  • No monitoring. Pipelines break. If you don't know, your CEO will before you do.

What good looks like

  • A dashboard your CEO opens every morning and trusts.
  • Every metric defined once, in one place - usually a metrics layer like dbt.
  • Data issues hit Slack before they hit leadership.
  • Your team is finding insights, not preparing reports.
  • New questions get answered in hours, not weeks.
